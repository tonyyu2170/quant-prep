**Goal:** Finish the `pure-math/*` family with its remaining two topics —
`pure-math/number-theory` (8) and `pure-math/solid-geometry` (6) — taking the bank from

**Why this batch:** `docs/research/quantguide-2026-08/FINDINGS.md:112` lists four strands under

**Tech Stack:** TypeScript templates + vitest gates; Python/numpy counterparts under
`verification/`; `npx tsx tools/probe.ts` for drafting measurements.

## DECIDED: solid geometry is drawn pi-FREE

Every solid-geometry template in this batch is designed so that no `\pi` is ever printed:
volumes come from boxes and prisms, and every curved solid is asked as a **fraction or ratio**,
where `\pi` cancels exactly.

This is not squeamishness about a macro. A `\pi` in a printed chain forces `\approx`, and
`\approx` makes the segment claim-free and unread — so a topic full of `\pi` would be a topic
the precision gate never checks. Ratios keep the arithmetic exact and the gate live.

## DECIDED: the Steinmetz solid is CUT, and it is not draftable

FINDINGS.md names it specifically, so its absence needs a reason on the record.

The bicylinder's volume is `16r^3/3`. **Every question about it is either proportional to `r^3`
— a ONE-axis draw space, which fails `maxRepeat <= 4` however many choices `r` gets — or a
constant ratio, which fails `distinct@band >= 12` outright.** The leftover inside the
circumscribing cube is `8r^3/3`, exactly half the solid; the cube is `3/2` of it. Both are
constants, independent of the draw.

This is the general hazard of the topic: **a solid has few dimensional parameters, so its answers
are low-dimensional.** Five of the six solid-geometry designs needed their choice lists widened
two- to fourfold before they cleared `texts/100 >= 70`, and the sixth had to give up an exact
answer entirely (below). Budget for that rather than discovering it at Task 3.

### `pure-math/number-theory` (8)

| # | id (after `pure-math/`) | d | answer | verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| N1 | `number-theory/multiples-in-a-range` | 1 | `floor(N/k) - floor(N/lcm(k,m))` | brute: count the range one integer at a time |
| N2 | `number-theory/coprime-count-two-primes` | 1 | `mult*(p-1)*(q-1)` | brute: test gcd on every integer in the span |
| N3 | `number-theory/gcd-lcm-product` | 1 | `g*m*n` | brute: smallest common multiple found by walking |
| N4 | `number-theory/frobenius-largest-unpayable` | 2 | `a*b - a - b` | brute: sieve every amount for representability |
| N5 | `number-theory/crt-two-congruences` | 2 | smallest n meeting both | brute: scan upward from 1 |
| N6 | `number-theory/diophantine-count-solutions` | 2 | count of nonneg (x,y) | brute: double loop over the lattice |
| N7 | `number-theory/linear-congruence-solve` | 3 | x with `a*x = r (mod m)` | brute: scan x from 1 to m |
| N8 | `number-theory/frobenius-fit-then-count` | 3 | fit b, then `(a-1)(b-1)/2` | brute: sieve and COUNT the unrepresentable |

Every brute route here is a **search or a sieve** — no closed form anywhere in the checker. That
is unusually strong independence, and it is why this topic is cheap to verify.

### `pure-math/solid-geometry` (6) — all pi-free

| # | id (after `pure-math/`) | d | answer | verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | `solid-geometry/volume-scaling-under-similarity` | 1 | `V*k^3` or `k^3` | brute: build a lattice solid, scale it, count cells |
| G2 | `solid-geometry/triangular-prism-volume` | 1 | `a*b*L/2` | brute: numeric integration over the cross-section |
| G3 | `solid-geometry/spherical-cap-fraction` | 2 | `h^2(3r-h)/(4r^3)` | brute: integrate the disc areas up the cap |
| G4 | `solid-geometry/cone-frustum-fraction` | 2 | `(R^3-r^3)/R^3` | brute: integrate the cone's cross-sections |
| G5 | `solid-geometry/displacement-water-level-rise` | 2 | `s^3/(a*b)` | brute: conservation of volume, solved numerically |
| G6 | `solid-geometry/box-fit-then-diagonal` | 3 | fit the third edge, then `sqrt(a^2+b^2+c^2)` | brute: maximise distance over the box's corners |

**G6 gave up an exact answer, deliberately.** It first required `a^2+b^2+c^2` to be a perfect
square so the diagonal came out whole. Pythagorean quadruples are rare enough that
`drawParams` threw `constraint unsatisfiable` — the rejection loop gives up after 100 attempts.
Dropping the requirement takes it from 52 tuples to 2280. The fit step stays exact (the third
edge is `V/(ab)` by construction); only the final root is irrational, and it takes `\approx`.

**N6 had to be constrained away from zero.** One legal draw asked for the number of ways to make
an amount that is not representable at all — a correct answer of 0, and a fixed point of the
relative perturbation the mutation check uses, so it would have shipped a verifier that
structurally cannot fail. The constraint now computes the count and rejects a draw of zero.
